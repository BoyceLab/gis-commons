# Awareness and introduction toolkit

When you meet a researcher, clinician, nonprofit leader or community organizer with a data or storytelling need, think of GeOALS. A useful introduction starts with the person's question and connects it to a specific capability.

## A short introduction

> GeOALS brings together mapping, real world evidence, electronic health records, environmental epidemiology, AI, visualization and web design to support the ALS community. We can help with a research question, build an interactive resource, tell a story with StoryMaps or train a team.

## Match the conversation to the need

| What someone says | A useful response |
|---|---|
| We want to study possible environmental associations | GeOALS can help define place and time, evaluate environmental data sources and plan a study that accounts for uncertainty and bias. |
| We have EHR or registry data but don't know what is missing | Our expertise includes real world evidence and record completeness. A first step could be a cohort definition and data quality assessment. |
| Our work is hard to explain to families or funders | A StoryMap or interactive web page can bring the narrative, images, video and findings together. Maps can be included where useful. |
| We want to use AI on narratives | Start with a clear task, appropriate data permissions and a validation plan with human review. |
| Our team needs practical skills | GeOALS experts can provide a workshop, short course or mentoring on StoryMaps, GIS and related research methods. |
| I'd like to help but I don't code | There are roles in community review, writing, source checking, accessibility, training and outreach. |

## Copyable introduction email

> Subject: A possible connection with GeOALS
>
> I thought of GeOALS when you described your work on [topic]. Its expertise includes geographic analysis, real world evidence, electronic health records, environmental epidemiology, AI, visualization and interactive storytelling. A useful starting point might be [a study design discussion / a StoryMaps consultation / a training session].
>
> You can learn about the work at https://www.geoals.org/ and contact Danielle Boyce at danielle@boycedatascience.com. If both of you are interested, I would be glad to make an introduction.

Ask permission before sharing someone's contact details or making a group introduction. Keep confidential project information out of an initial message.

## A message for an organization with a story to tell

> Do you have a story that deserves more than a static page? GeOALS can advise on or help create an ArcGIS StoryMap: an interactive narrative combining text, photos, video, audio, charts and, where useful, maps. We can help plan the story, build it with your team or teach you how to maintain it.

## A small outreach plan

1. Identify one audience: a research team, clinic, advocacy organization, student group or community partner.
2. Choose one relevant example, such as a [StoryMap](../maps/storymaps.md), [training topic](../grants/training.md) or [environmental study example](../environment/examples.md).
3. Offer a specific next step: a conversation about a question, a demonstration, a training discussion or a volunteer task.
4. Follow up only with permission. Record the topic and agreed next step without collecting health information.

## Materials to prepare for an event

- A link or QR code to the public GeOALS website or this public site's Work and volunteer page
- A short introduction and one example relevant to the audience
- The [services overview](services.md), [volunteer roles](volunteer.md) and contact details
- Permission for any person's name, quote, photograph or story used in materials
- A way to distinguish general inquiries, volunteer inquiries and future research participation

Use current public pages when creating a QR code. Do not link to the private source repository. Do not collect a list of people with ALS or residential histories at an awareness event; any future study intake belongs in the approved secure workflow.
