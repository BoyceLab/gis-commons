# Volunteer with GeOALS

You do not need to code or be a GIS specialist to contribute. People living with ALS, caregivers, bereaved loved ones, students and professionals bring different kinds of knowledge. We can begin with a bounded task and a clear point of contact.

## Find a role

The time ranges below are examples for planning, not minimum commitments.

| Role | A first task | Helpful experience | Example time |
|---|---|---|---|
| Community reviewer | Review one page or a fictional map exercise for clarity and emotional tone | Lived experience, caregiving or community knowledge | 30–60 minutes |
| Resource researcher | Check a short list of public links and record source dates | Careful reading and source checking | 1–2 hours |
| Story contributor or editor | Outline a story, review a paragraph or help identify media permissions | Writing, editing or storytelling | 1–3 hours |
| Accessibility reviewer | Test keyboard navigation, labels, contrast or assistive technology use | Accessibility experience or lived expertise | 1–3 hours |
| Mapper or analyst | Prepare a documented example using public or synthetic data | GIS, statistics or environmental methods | 2–4 hours per week |
| Data and informatics contributor | Review a dictionary, terminology mapping or reproducibility guide | EHR, real world evidence, coding or standards | A defined small project |
| AI evaluation contributor | Check extracted statements against a synthetic or approved source set | Research methods, informatics or subject knowledge | A defined review batch |
| Designer or developer | Improve a page, chart, accessible form or demo interaction | Web, visual or interaction design | A defined feature |
| Trainer or mentor | Teach a session, review an exercise or support a student | Subject expertise and teaching interest | Preparation plus a session |
| Outreach connector | Share the public resource or make an introduction with permission | Community, clinical, research or nonprofit connections | 30–60 minutes |

## How volunteering works

1. **Introduce yourself.** Email [Danielle Boyce](mailto:danielle@boycedatascience.com) with your interests, skills, availability and any access needs. A CV is optional.
2. **Agree on a first task.** Define the purpose, output, time estimate, reviewer and a reasonable completion date.
3. **Use appropriate materials.** Most volunteer tasks can use public or synthetic information. Access to restricted study data requires a separately approved role, training, agreements and the minimum necessary permissions.
4. **Review together.** A designated reviewer checks accuracy, accessibility and permissions before anything is published.
5. **Choose what comes next.** Continue, try another task or stop. A small contribution is useful on its own.

## Participation that respects people's circumstances

Tasks can be asynchronous, broken into shorter sessions or completed with communication support. Bereaved contributors can pause without explaining why. We do not ask volunteers to disclose their own medical history or a loved one's history to qualify.

Discuss acknowledgment, authorship criteria, expenses and any compensated role before work begins. Authorship depends on the contribution and the publication's requirements. A research participant advisory role, a paid consulting role and a volunteer task should each have clear expectations; do not assume they are interchangeable.

Volunteers should not independently recruit research participants, obtain consent, access identifiers or contact families using study information. Those activities require assignment and training under an approved study process.
