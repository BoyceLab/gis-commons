## About GeoALS

**Mission**, based on the mission statement on the GeoALS website

> GeoALS uses geographic information systems (GIS) to improve care, accelerate research, and advocate for the ALS community.

**Tools**

> GeoALS maintains the ALS Geospatial Hub, which brings together data from federal agencies, research institutions and nonprofit organizations by geography; the ALS Clinic Advisor, a locator and community review system for ALS and motor neuron disease clinics; and the Glen Rouse ALS Legislative Viewer, which reports each member of Congress's support for ALS policy. Its maps are built by the Mappers, a volunteer group of GIS professionals, ALS researchers and advocates.

A fuller organizational description is to be discussed with GeoALS. Background on the organization is on the [GeoALS website](https://www.geoals.org/).

**Research experience**

> GeoALS has provided geographic analysis to research partners including the CDC National ALS Registry, where it mapped self-enrolled survey data to study access to clinical trials, common lived locations and under-reported communities; the ALS Therapy Development Institute, where it has studied the place history of natural history study participants and patterns of recruitment; and the Team Gleason Foundation, where it identified communities most in need of services.

## Approach paragraphs

**Access to care**

> We will measure geographic access to multidisciplinary ALS care using the GeoALS clinic database, which is maintained with national and international ALS organizations. For each <span class="fill">[participant / census tract]</span> we will calculate travel time by road to the nearest clinic and describe how access varies by <span class="fill">[rurality / region / other characteristic]</span>. Results will be reported for areas, with small counts suppressed.

**Recruitment and representativeness**

> To assess how well enrollment reflects the population affected, GeoALS will compare the residential distribution of participants with <span class="fill">[reference distribution, for example registry or population-based estimates]</span> at the <span class="fill">[state / county]</span> level, and will identify areas where enrollment is lower than expected. Findings will guide outreach during the enrollment period.

**Residential history and environmental context**

> Participants' residential histories will be geocoded within <span class="fill">[the lead institution's secure environment]</span>. GeoALS will design and document the linkage of each residence period to open environmental and neighborhood data sets, producing person-period variables for analysis. The linkage code and a data dictionary will be published so that other cohorts can repeat the method. No addresses or coordinates will leave the secure environment.

**Standards**

> Location history and linked environmental variables will be stored using the OMOP Common Data Model with the extension tables developed by the OHDSI GIS working group, and coded with open ontologies where terms exist. Where a needed term is missing, the team will submit a request to the relevant ontology so that the work benefits later studies.

**Dissemination through maps**

> Findings will be shared with the ALS community through an interactive story map and dashboard on the ALS Geospatial Hub, written in plain language and reviewed by people living with ALS before release.

## Facilities and resources

> GeoALS's public tools are built on the ArcGIS platform, including ArcGIS Online for hosted data and web maps, ArcGIS Hub for public sites, ArcGIS Experience Builder and ArcGIS Dashboards for applications, ArcGIS Survey123 for data collection, and ArcGIS StoryMaps for narrative reporting. The ALS Geospatial Hub hosts GeoALS's public maps, data layers and applications, including a clinic database covering the United States and other countries. For projects that involve identifiable data, GeoALS personnel will work inside the lead institution's secure computing environment under that institution's agreements, and no identifiable data will be stored on GeoALS systems.

## Human subjects and privacy

> Home addresses and coordinates derived from them are treated as direct identifiers. Geocoding will take place within the secure environment of the institution holding the data. GeoALS personnel who need access to study information will be listed by the institution's institutional review board (IRB) as approved study personnel. Analysts will work with derived variables wherever the research question allows, and all maps and tables will be reviewed by a person for disclosure risk before release.
